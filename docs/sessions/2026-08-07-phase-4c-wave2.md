## 1. Scoping and the split

4C as originally scoped (2026-08-05 record §17) was one phase: bloom's
maturity payload plus all four remaining coagulant kinds. Claude flagged
before planning that this was meaningfully bigger than 3C, which had
needed its own playtest-and-fix round afterward — 3C shipped three kinds
where everything was "same formula, different numbers." Full 4C is four
kinds *plus* two structurally novel mechanics: Carrier writing back into
the grid as it travels, and Bulwark being non-circular when every damage,
collision, targeting and render path assumed a circle.

**Split along that seam**, agreed with the owner:

- **4C-1** — bloom's maturity payload, armour from maturity, Sclerotic,
  Blastoma, a +50% weapon damage pass. No new machinery — identity rules
  layered on mechanics that already existed.
- **4C-2** — Carrier and Bulwark, the pair §10 requires shipped together,
  plus the multi-part body model both need.

**The owner pre-authorized the whole arc in one instruction**: build 4C-1,
verify it live, and if it read clean, self-greenlight 4C-2 without waiting
for another round-trip — *"if you find all tests clear and you're able to
visually verify 4C-1 working well, I greenlight 4C-2. Then test it and
report success or failure of full 4th phase."* Both sub-phases were built,
tested, and verified in the same sitting.

## 2. Four design calls, made before any code

**Blastoma splits at 50% of its starting mass**, not at death. The owner's
proposal — *"two little motes that split the leftover mass"* — was refined
in the plan: splitting on death is impossible, since by then mass is 0 and
there's nothing left to give the children, which would break Rule 2
(killing is a sink). A threshold split conserves exactly instead.

**Fragment kind is derived from mass, not hard-coded.** The plan flagged
that "two little motes" wasn't literally guaranteed — Rule 4 derivation
usually yields *congealers* at realistic split masses, and hard-coding
`'mote'` would be the design's first spawn-table exception. The owner
clarified the "motes" framing wasn't a hard requirement: *"what I meant
wasn't supposed to read like a hardcode, but more like an example... I
agree with deriving it from the mass."* The owner also named a real future
idea while agreeing — at some point the project may need to move from
"events are the only spark" toward something closer to a spawn table, or a
merge of the two — explicitly **not now**, logged for whenever the roster
needs it.

**Tune gently, and raise weapon damage 50%.** 4C-1 adds the game's first
nonzero armour while its designed counter (Phase 5's penetration) doesn't
exist. The owner: *"the game is quite hard as of now, so tune it gently
and maybe tune up the damage the weapons do by 50%, so we can see our
things implemented working but also not be overwhelmed [within] 30 seconds
of gameplay."* Implemented as one `WEAPON_DAMAGE_SCALE` constant
multiplied into all six weapons' damage functions rather than editing
twelve coefficients by hand.

**Projectile-blocking (open question 4) stays out of 4C**, moved to
BACKLOG's *Ideas* section with its reasoning intact rather than left
dangling as a phase-scoped open question with no phase left to close it.

## 3. 4C-1: identity gets a third and fourth reading

§10 derives coagulant identity from four field readings. 3C only used one
(mass); 4A/4B built the terrain that makes a second (maturity) legible but
nothing read it yet. 4C-1 wires in maturity and a new one, mass *shape*;
4C-2 adds the fourth, corridor density.

**Mass shape** rides the existing flood-fill traversal — no second pass.
`floodFillMass` now also tracks the furthest cell visited and compares
cells actually reached against the area of the disc that reach implies:

```
fillRatio = cells.length / (π · maxDist² / cellSize²)
```

Near 1 for a solid saturated patch; low for a thin corridor that reaches
far while visiting few cells — exactly §10's "webbed through an area"
description of what produces a Blastoma.

**Identity composes in a deliberate order**: maturity beats everything
(*"my own kill zone got up and walked at me"* should hold regardless of
mass or shape), split further into Bulwark vs. plain Sclerotic by a mass
bar (`MASS_BULWARK`, reusing `MASS_BEHEMOTH`); then corridor density for
Carrier; then fragmented shape at sufficient mass for Blastoma; then the
ordinary Wave 1 mass tiers. 4C-1 deliberately left the high-mass+scarred
table cell falling through to Sclerotic, flagged in code as staged rather
than forgotten — 4C-2 is what actually splits it.

**Armour is a function of source maturity, never a per-kind table** — a
Sclerotic is armoured *because* it formed from hardened ground, so no
separate balancing pass is needed as kinds are added. `COAGULANT_ARMOR_FLOOR`
(Decision 44) has been live since 3C waiting for exactly this to feed it a
nonzero value.

## 4. Two balance bugs, found the same way as every phase since 4A

Both were invisible to any test written *before* running the game, and
both were caught by the same deterministic debug-harness methodology
(Decision 59, prescribed by the owner back at the 3C playtest gate): grant
max weapons, disable XP, fast-forward past hundreds of seconds, then look
at what the roster actually produced.

**Bug 1 — blooms couldn't spark their own Sclerotic.** §11 says blooms
exist specifically to let armour *"appear mid-field, earlier, as a
discrete event."* First pass sized `BLOOM_MATURITY_ACTIVE_RATE`/`PEAK_RATE`
against active+peak combined (~7.5s). But a bloom's own formation attempt
fires at the *instant* peak begins (`advancePhase` arms `formationTimer`
to 0 exactly then) — so only the 4s active window has actually accumulated
anything by the time a bloom tries to spark itself. At the original rate,
that reached ~0.16 maturity at the epicenter. Retuned ~4x, to reach ~0.6 at
the exact center before flood-fill dilution.

**Bug 2 — `MATURITY_SCLEROTIC_THRESHOLD` (0.55) was never reachable.**
Formation reads *mean* maturity over the whole flood-filled footprint, and
that mean dilutes hard toward the surrounding region's average. A single
grid cell can scar to ~0.97 under sustained combat — but across a 500s
max-weapons run, even after fixing bug 1, the highest mean any coagulant
ever actually sparked at was ~0.46. **Zero Sclerotics formed in that
entire run.** Lowered to 0.4, comfortably above `AGE_CEILING` (0.33, so
passive aging still can't trigger it alone) and below what was empirically
observed as reachable. After both fixes: 5 of 8 active coagulants in one
run were Sclerotic, armour scaling correctly, rendering in the correct
pale palette.

**The pattern holding across four phases now (4A, 4B, 4C-1) is the same
one**: a plausible-sounding number, sized against an assumption that
doesn't survive contact with the actual simulation, caught only by
watching the real thing run rather than by unit tests exercising the
formula in isolation.

## 5. 4C-2: the architecture question — a cluster of circles

Bulwark is specified as *"wide and flat rather than round: a moving
wall."* Every existing coagulant system assumes a single circle: `clearAt`'s
damage loop (`circleOverlapArea`), `findCoagulantHit`'s distance check,
`nearestFrontierPoint`'s surface-distance targeting, and the seed-blob
renderer.

**Chosen: model a body as a cluster of circles**, offset from a centre.
`radius` stays the bounding circle for every existing cheap broad-phase
reject, unmodified; when `parts` is populated, narrow-phase iterates the
actual parts instead. Absent/empty `parts` — every Wave 1 kind, Sclerotic,
Blastoma, Carrier — is exactly today's single-circle behaviour, unchanged.

Two alternatives were considered and rejected in the plan: approximating
Bulwark as one bigger circle loses the "wall" read entirely (the shape
*is* the mechanic, since it's what lets it screen whatever's behind it);
true ellipse geometry needs a new overlap-area formula to sit alongside
`circleOverlapArea`, real added complexity for a first-pass body shape
this simple. The cluster approach reuses every piece of circle math
already written and tested, and composes for free with Blastoma's existing
seed-based "bag of blobs" read.

**Two shared primitives** (`systems/coagulants.ts`) replace what four call
sites used to compute inline: `coagulantSurfaceDist` (nearest-part
distance, used by targeting and hit detection) and `coagulantOverlapArea`
(summed per-part overlap, used by damage). One accepted simplification,
documented in code: overlapping parts aren't de-duplicated, so a hit
landing where two parts overlap can be counted slightly more than once.
Kept modest by the part-radius/spacing constants; proper silhouette-union
math would be real complexity for a body this simple.

**Carrier** feeds off the field it crosses — the hook Decision 42 left in
place for exactly this back in 3C (*"cheap to leave, annoying to
retrofit"*). Consumes revealed growth in a small radius each tick, adds it
to its own mass, updates its own radius and speed to match, capped
relative to its own starting mass so it can't compound without bound. Its
formation gate is the fourth and last §10 reading: mean revealed density
sampled along the straight line from spark point to core — a pure
failure-gate, unaffected by mass or maturity at the spark point itself.

## 6. Carrier and Bulwark, live

Verified with the same debug-harness methodology. A 600s max-weapons run
produced **7 Bulwarks** with correct armour, 4-part bodies, and a bounding
radius that actually enclosed every part — visually confirmed as pale,
bone-coloured, elongated shapes, clearly distinct from round pink
Behemoths. **No Carrier formed** under max weapons, which is the design
working as intended: max weapons keep the near field clean, so no thick
corridor ever exists to feed one — *"a good player never meets one"* (§10).
A weaker-loadout run (starting weapon only, 90s to near-death) also
produced no Carrier; the corridor-density bar is a genuinely demanding
one (mean density the *entire* way to the core, not just patchy), so its
rarity is plausible rather than concerning. The mechanic itself is
confirmed correct by construction: `coagulantKindFrom`'s corridor branch is
directly unit-tested, and the full `attemptFormation` integration is
tested end-to-end against hand-calculated flood-fill drainage geometry —
both passed on the first run, which is what gave confidence to not chase a
rarer live sighting further.

## 8. Ideas considered and rejected

| Idea | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **Ship 4C as one phase** | Bigger than 3C, which needed its own playtest-and-fix round. Split along the real seam: identity-only vs. new-machinery. |
| **Blastoma splits on death** | By death, mass is 0 — nothing left to give children, and inventing some breaks Rule 2. Splits at a mass threshold instead, conserving exactly. |
| **Hard-code fragment kind as 'mote'** | Rule 4's first spawn-table exception. Derived from mass instead; the owner's "motes" was the fantasy, not a requirement. |
| **Tune armour at full strength for this gate** | Its counter (penetration) is Phase 5 and doesn't exist. Gentle armour + 50% weapon damage instead, so the mechanic is visible without ending runs in 30 seconds. |
| **Approximate Bulwark as one larger circle** | Loses the "wall" read entirely — the shape is what lets it screen what's behind it. |
| **True ellipse geometry for Bulwark** | Needs a new overlap-area formula alongside `circleOverlapArea`; real complexity for a first-pass body this simple. Cluster-of-circles reuses everything already written. |
| **De-duplicate overlapping parts' damage area** | Proper silhouette-union math, for a body shape (a short line of 4 circles) where the actual over-count is small. Documented as a known simplification instead. |
| **Chase a live Carrier sighting further** | The mechanic is unit-tested end-to-end against hand-calculated geometry and passed cleanly; rarity under both tested loadouts is plausible given how demanding the corridor bar is, not a sign of a broken formation gate. |
